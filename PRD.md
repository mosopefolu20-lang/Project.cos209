# Babcock FoodEase
## 1. Problem Statement

Students experience difficulties when purchasing food on campus due to the closure of several available outlets. This has resulted in increased concertration at the remaining outlets which is causing a very high demand of products without a supply rate to match it.It has led to overcrowding, longer waiting times and discomfort during the food buying process. Consequently students have fewer options when trying to purchase meals especially during busy periods.

## 2. Project Overview

Babcock FoodEase is a proposed multi-vendor food ordering platform designed for students at Babcock University. This platform will allow students to view different food vendors, browse available food and prices, place orders and make payments before visiting the selected vendor to collect their food order.
The aim is to make food purchasing easier and much more convenient and reduce unnecessary waiting.

## 3. Target Users
### 3.1 Students
Students are the primary users of Babcock FoodEase. They will use the platform to browse available food vendors, view prices and menus, place food orders, make online payments and receive a notification when the order is ready for collection.

### 3.2 Food Vendors
They will use the platform to manage their food menus, update prices and availability, receive orders and provide estimated preparation times.

### 3.3 Administrator
The administrator will oversee the platform, manage user and vendor accounts and ensure the system works properly.
 
 ## 4. User Research
 ### 4.1 Research Method
 Primary research was conducted through interviews with 4 Babcock students. The interviews focused on students waiting times when purchasing food, their frequently visited food vendors, their awerness of the closure of food outlets, how the closure has affected the food buying experience and their willingness to use a food ordering system.

 ### 4.2 Interview Findings 
 1. Waiting Time: Students reported different waiting times depending on  how busy the food stores were. Reported waiting times ranged from 3-5 minutes during queiter periods to 20-30 mins or longer during busy periods. One student specifically said waiting could take up to an hour.
 2. Food Outlet Closures: Students confirmed noticing that some previously available food ooulets had been shut down.
 3. Limited Food Choices: Some students explained that the closure of their preferred vendors had reduced their available food choices.
 4. Overcrowding: This is the main issue, Students reported fewer available food outlets have contributed to more students gathering around specific vendors.
 5. Interest in Advanced Ordering:The students expresssed varying levels of interest in using a system that allows them to order food before arriving at their specified vendor. However, some prefer visiting the shop physically or seeing the food before ordering.

 ### 4.3 Research Limitations
 The research involved 4 students participants. No food vendors were interviewed due to difficuties accessing them. Therefore, the findings primarily reflect students experiences and expectations. Further research with food vendors will be necessary to validate the proposed systems operational requirements.

 ## 5. Existing Solutions and Current practices

 Currently, students purchase food by visiting food vendors physically and placing their orders at the shops. They depend on the available food oulets to meet their daily food needs.
 However, following the closure of some food oulets, students have fewer places to purchase meals. This has contribited to overcrowding and longer waiting times at the remaining outlets, particularly during busy hours. 

 ### 5.1 Limitations of Current Practices
 The current physical purchasing process has some limitations:
 1. Students spend considerable time waiting in queues.
 2. Fewer available outlets limits students choices.
 2. Students may experience overcrowding during busy  periods.
 4. Students cannot conveniently place orders through the physical walk-in process described in the interviews.

 ## 6. Proposed Solutions
 Babcock FoodEase is proposed as a centralized, multi-vendor food ordering platform that will provide students with access to different food vendors through one application.
 Students will be able to browse vendors, view available meals, check prices and place orders before visiting their selected vendors for collection.
 The platform will allow students to:
 1. Browse different food vendors in one application.
 2. View food menus, pictures, descriptions and prices.
 3. Add multiple food items from one vendor to a single cart.
 4. Make online payments for their orders. 
 5. Receive estimated food preparation times from vendors.
 6. Receive notifications when their orders are ready for collection.
 7. Cancel orders before food preparation begins.

 Food vendors will be able to manage their menus, update food availability, receive orders and update order statuses. 
 An aadministrator will oversee the platform, manage accounts and handle reported issues.
 The main goal of Babcock FoodEase is to improve convenience, reduce unnecessary waiting and make food ordering more organised for students and vendors.

## 7. MVP Scope
The Minimum Viable Product of Babcock FoodEase will focus on the essential  features required to make food ordering more convenient for students.

### 7.1 Features Included
1. Student registration and login.
2. Vendor registration and login.
3. Display of available food vendors.
4. Food menus with pictures, description and prices.
5. Shopping cart for multiple items from one vendor.
6. Online payment for orders.
7. Vendor management of food availability.
8. Order acceptance and estimated preparation time.
9. Order status updates
10. Notifications when orders are ready for collection
11. Order cancellation before preparation begins.
12. Administrator dashboard for platform oversight
13. Pickup confirmation

### 7.2 Features Excluded from the MVP
The  following may be considered for future versions:
1. Food delivery services
2. Customer reviews and ratings
3. AI based food recommendation.

## 8. Success Metrics
1. Reduced waiting time: Compare students waiting time before and after using the platform
2. Order completion rate: Measure the percentage of orders successfully completed and collected
3. Student Satisfaction: Gather feedback from students about their experience using the application 
4. Vendor participation: Monitor the number of food vendors actively using the platforms
5. System Reliability: Monitor failed orders, payment errors and system downtime.

## 9. Risks and limitations
The following explain thethings that could make Babcock FoodEase difficult to develop:
1. Internet connectivity might be unreliable
2. Online payments might fail
3. Vendors might struggle to manage online orders alongside walk in customers.
4. Vendors might forget to update food availability.
